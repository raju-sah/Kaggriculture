# V62 mechanics audit: shops and fertilizer

## Finding

Installed `kaggle-environments` 1.32.7 agrees with its shipped README on shop demand when the terms are separated: each unlocked shop instance consumes a fixed amount at each sell interval; total shop demand grows in steps as instances unlock, with duplicate shops allowed. Fertilizer lasts for the current day and the following two days, but it does not generally double an entire crop's yield. It adds one extra unit only at eligible watered events: one-time crops get +2 instead of +1 in their crop-specific watering window, and ongoing crops produce +2 rather than +1 on a scheduled production day if they are fertilized and watered that day.

The v57 replay has many ineffective fertilizer commands, but the two clearest missed wheat opportunities lacked accessible fertilizer. A separate narrow intervention is mechanically possible: replace a duplicate `WATER` command that is already guaranteed to no-op with `FERTILIZE`, when the same unit carries fertilizer and the crop is in its one-time bonus window. This keeps the worker in the same place and does not undo the watering already completed that day. The later water schedule is not known from the current observation, however, and the replay-only mark-to-market estimate is mixed or negative across these regimes. No candidate or fresh game screen is recommended yet.

## Installed engine and README

The audited engine is `.venv/lib/python3.12/site-packages/kaggle_environments/envs/kaggriculture/kaggriculture.py`, SHA-256
`bc8a54879ef02c7ea64b8b333d6a976f0ea65c4949149d01f463f23bccee653e`.

`SHOPS` defines the products consumed by each shop (`kaggriculture.py:103–112`). `_town_consume` removes one unit per demanded product for every unlocked shop instance every `townShopSellInterval` turns, and doubles consumption for single-product shops (`:728–749`). `_end_of_day` draws another shop uniformly with replacement every three days by default, until eight instances (`:860–892`). Thus an individual instance's demand is flat, while aggregate demand grows in steps as the town adds instances. The installed README states the same behavior at `README.md:169–173`. The discussion's claimed engine/README divergence was not reproduced on this installed build; it may refer to a different package revision or to confusing flat per-instance demand with changing aggregate demand. The discussion page rendered no body text in the available browser view, so this audit treats its claim as a reported hypothesis.

The plant table gives first-yield timing, intervals, and caps (`kaggriculture.py:11–16`). `FERTILIZE` consumes one carried fertilizer and sets `fertilized_until_day` to at least `day + 2` (`:475–482`). In other words, the flag is active on the action day and the next two days. For one-time crops, `WATER` is eligible from `ceil(max_yield_day / 2)` through `max_yield_day`, and adds two units if fertilized, versus one without fertilizer (`:431–444`; README `:123–124`). For ongoing crops, daily refresh grants two units only if the crop is on its scheduled production date, it was watered that day, and fertilizer is active (`:769–803`; README `:125`). Fertilizing alone does not create output, and the ongoing-crop effect is conditional on watering.

Unit actions are processed one at a time for each worker; invalid actions silently no-op (`kaggriculture.py:312–318`). In particular, `WATER` immediately returns if the plant is already watered today (`:431–436`), and `FERTILIZE` immediately returns if that worker lacks fertilizer (`:475–481`). The worker action has one operation per unit per turn, not a retry queue. Market orders use a separate queue: an order is cancelled when a unit commit fails (`:555–628`). This distinction means a reported queue-blocker/retry improvement from a Kaggle participant cannot be applied to worker actions without separate evidence. The separate participant-reported +$4,373 margin in discussion [742856](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/kaggriculture/discussion/742856) is anecdotal here; its body was also unavailable in the browser view.

## Saved v57 replay audit

I inspected the candidate-seat (`ab`, seat 0) traces for v50 seeds 95501, 95508, and 95518 in `experiments/v59_state_teacher/dev95501_95519/replays/`. These share the v57 worker route; teacher diagnostics record identical farmer/hand actions across the sampled win/loss replays.

Each trace contains 149 `FERTILIZE` commands. In each trace, 99 had a plant and carried fertilizer, but the observed tile was already fertilized through at least `day + 2`; because the engine uses `max(old_until, day + 2)`, these commands did not extend the window. Forty-seven targeted a plant but that worker had no fertilizer. In 45 of those cases the tile was already fertilized through `day + 2`; two were genuine unserved windows on wheat at steps 477 and 479 in seed 95508 (day 19, plants at `(0,7)` and `(1,7)`, planted days 17 and 18). Neither the actor inventory nor shed had fertilizer then. The recorded route watered those plants later, but there is no immediate retry path: fertilizer cannot be conjured from another worker's private inventory, and shed pickup requires standing adjacent to the shed (`:358–375`). Three further fertilizer commands referred to hand indices with no active hand position and therefore no-op (`:278–282`, `:312–318`).

A more promising state-level no-op was visible in these same traces: the worker sometimes received `WATER` on a plant whose `watered_today` flag was already true, while carrying fertilizer. An offline scan found 50 such unique opportunities per trace after avoiding repeated fertilization of the same plant while its two-day extension remained active. They are mechanically safe with respect to current watering and route position: substituting `FERTILIZE` leaves the worker on the same tile, and does not remove water already applied. A replay-only calculation using subsequent watering visible in the saved tape estimated 33 extra crop units across those 50 substitutions in each trace. At the contemporaneous spot prices, estimated extra crop value versus the spot value of fertilizer was $1,973 versus $1,926 on seed 95501, $1,740 versus $1,926 on seed 95508, and $1,815 versus $1,927 on seed 95518. These are not simulated final coin deltas: they assume the future saved route remains unchanged and price incremental goods at current spot prices. The mixed/negative estimates do not establish a profitable intervention.

## Bounded proposal and decision

A future isolated candidate could test only this rule: if the planner emits `WATER` for a one-time plant already watered today, the current worker carries fertilizer, the plant is in its crop-specific bonus window, the plant has yield headroom, and `fertilized_until_day < day + 2`, replace that no-op `WATER` with `FERTILIZE`. Keep every other action unchanged. This condition preserves the current day's water and worker position. Its value depends on future watering before the fertilizer expires; if none occurs, it spends an item without benefit. A production test should first use an offline action-diff replay and include paired frozen-reset controls; this audit does not authorize or run those games.

No code, agent, or submission files were changed, and no new games were run. The official source behavior verifies the mechanic but does not yet justify promoting a fertilizer overlay.
