# V62 Kaggriculture discussion and mechanics research

**Checked:** 2026-09-26. Kaggle pages often render relative dates (for example, “10 days ago”); dates below preserve that label at retrieval rather than infer exact publication timestamps. This is a research note, not evidence that any proposed policy change wins.

## Findings that should shape the next experiments

1. **Use the live engine version as ground truth, especially after balance patches.** Kaggle staff announced an important market patch: the town center now consumes one unit of each configured product once per day (no late-season multiplier), and shops are sampled *with replacement*. A shop can therefore repeat, and each repeated copy consumes independently. The staff post says to update to `kaggle-environments >= 1.32.6`; a participant later flags price-curve changes and recommends pinning `1.32.7`. These are not minor details: a strategy tuned against the old shop schedule or old price curves is for a different game. [Kaggle staff: Balance Changes, posted about a month before retrieval](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/kaggriculture/discussion/733431); [participant version warning, posted 10 days before retrieval](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/kaggriculture/discussion/737459); [official engine source](https://github.com/Kaggle/kaggle-environments/blob/master/kaggle_environments/envs/kaggriculture/kaggriculture.py).

   **Mechanics verified in current source:** the end-of-day RNG is recreated from the episode seed and day; it is consumed while spawning weeds across both farms and then for the shop draw. The source loops over each empty unlocked tile and draws once per tile. Since an agent's actions change how many empty tiles its farm has, a changed policy can perturb the subsequent shop sequence. This supports the replay-evaluation warning below. The engine also processes shop demand per unlocked shop instance, once each configured shop interval; duplicate shop instances count separately. [Engine `_spawn_weeds`, `_end_of_day`, and `_town_consume`](https://github.com/Kaggle/kaggle-environments/blob/master/kaggle_environments/envs/kaggriculture/kaggriculture.py).

2. **Replay-on-recorded-opponent is a confounded test when the tested policy changes the market/RNG trajectory.** In the RL discussion, a participant ranked 62nd recommends evaluating each seed in both seat orders and averaging the pair, plus keeping an untouched seed block. They report a +$2,301 change on one “fresh” block and −$1,259 on another after selection bias. More specifically, they note that weed spawning consumes RNG draws on both farms: changing one side's play can alter later shop unlocks, so a recorded opponent's fixed actions may no longer be responding to the shop world it originally saw. Their reported top-10 win rates were 78% in “re-rolled” tests and 40% when the shop sequence was preserved; these are participant measurements, not independently reproduced results. The engine implementation confirms the RNG coupling mechanism. [“How are people making RL work in Kaggriculture?”, posted 10 days before retrieval, comment by Syed Asad Ali](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/kaggriculture/discussion/740792); [engine source](https://github.com/Kaggle/kaggle-environments/blob/master/kaggle_environments/envs/kaggriculture/kaggriculture.py).

   **Practical implication:** for ranking candidate agents, prefer paired matches on genuine seeds with both seats and live reactive opponents, and hold out seeds before tuning. Recorded replays are excellent for describing actions, cash flows, and policy fingerprints; counterfactual replay outcomes should be labeled as non-reactive diagnostics. A preserved-vs-rerolled split can help explain a replay result, but neither alone substitutes for live paired matches.

3. **A fertilizer timing gate is plausible and source-checkable.** A participant reports that `FERTILIZE` doubles yield only for a crop production event within the current day through day+2 inclusive and says adding a “production in day..day+2” gate raised their fertilize count from 0 to 54. Current engine source sets `fertilized_until_day = max(existing, day + 2)`, and crop production checks the active fertilization window. Thus the three-day inclusive window is real. The reported 0→54 action count and performance benefit remain that participant’s own results; more actions do not by themselves establish higher income. [“My Journey So Far — Mechanics, Breakdowns, and What's Actually Working,” posted about 3 days before retrieval](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/kaggriculture/discussion/740847); [current engine source](https://github.com/Kaggle/kaggle-environments/blob/master/kaggle_environments/envs/kaggriculture/kaggriculture.py).

4. **Stop optimizing only terminal coin margin.** The contest is finally ranked by win/loss outcomes in a final Bradley–Terry tournament, not coin margin. A recent participant reports two candidates over 384 paired games each: one had +$27,682 mean margin / 91.7% wins, the other +$25,715 / 97.7%; the lower-margin policy won their head-to-head by 6 percentage points. That example is a participant claim, but it matches Kaggle's official description that final ranks use a Bradley–Terry tournament on episodes. Optimize and report paired win rate with uncertainty, using margin and cash-flow diagnostics to explain changes. [Kaggle staff: final evaluation method](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/kaggriculture/discussion/731587); [Kaggle Overview: Evaluation](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/kaggriculture/overview); [“What actually predicted the ladder, and 15 things that didn't,” posted 2 days before retrieval](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/kaggriculture/discussion/742856).

5. **Execution failures can be strategic losses.** In that same latest discussion, the author reports that queueing a blocked action, clearing the blocker, and replaying the action won +$4,373 mean margin (confidence interval +$3,345 to +$5,339) in their paired real-ladder-board measurements. This is an actionable audit target for our agent: inspect invalid/no-op actions and worker assignments that pass over an immediately available task. The measurement comes from one participant's harness and agent; reproduce locally before adopting the rule. [Discussion 742856](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/kaggriculture/discussion/742856).

6. **Static plans explain why replay cloning can plateau, and why adaptivity should be tested specifically against repeated patterns.** In “Can a truly closed-loop strategy really outperform an open-loop strategy?”, commenters say the current meta is a stable open-loop asset-development tape and claim such tapes reached about 81% win rate in their experience; the same discussion argues the “static meta” has a ceiling and that market reactivity is more complex. A top-121 commenter in a newer thread describes their own RL policy as learning a repeated pattern and says it is exploitable in some matchups. These are informed opinions/anecdotes, not proof that RL or a reactive policy is stronger. A defensible next move is to identify the opponent's repeated schedule from replays, formulate explicit adversarial tests, and measure against an actual opponent panel rather than assume either architecture wins. [“Can a truly closed-loop strategy really outperform an open-loop strategy?”, posted about a month before retrieval](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/kaggriculture/discussion/733002); [“A question about players using the same pattern,” posted 2 days before retrieval](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/kaggriculture/discussion/741792).

## Mechanics notes from the authoritative source

- Each farmer/hand gets one unit action per turn; market orders are separately queued. The current source atomically rejects *all* `PLANT` actions for a crop if the combined worker requests exceed available seeds. A seed allocator must therefore match buys to same-turn aggregate plant intents, not just check each worker independently. [Engine source](https://github.com/Kaggle/kaggle-environments/blob/master/kaggle_environments/envs/kaggriculture/kaggriculture.py).
- Current source says shop unlocks are sampled with replacement, consumes once every configured four turns, and town-center drain defaults to one item per product every 24 turns. This supersedes the older README wording that said center demand doubles after day 10 and quadruples after day 20. In contrast, current source also confirms fertilization lasts through `day + 2` and a care bonus banks only when the animal is both cared for and fed. Check the exact installed package/release used for every experiment; do not combine numbers from pre- and post-patch runs. [Current engine source](https://github.com/Kaggle/kaggle-environments/blob/master/kaggle_environments/envs/kaggriculture/kaggriculture.py); [older README text illustrating the conflict](https://github.com/Kaggle/kaggle-environments/blob/master/kaggle_environments/envs/kaggriculture/README.md); [staff change notice](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/kaggriculture/discussion/733431).
- Official README/source notes a 100-unit shed capacity (excluding seeds) and that unsold inventory does not count toward terminal bank/reward. That makes pickup/selling throughput and end-game liquidation part of the economic policy, rather than presentation details. Verify against the pinned engine version before basing exact capacity/timing on it. [Official engine README](https://github.com/Kaggle/kaggle-environments/blob/master/kaggle_environments/envs/kaggriculture/README.md); [official engine source](https://github.com/Kaggle/kaggle-environments/blob/master/kaggle_environments/envs/kaggriculture/kaggriculture.py).

## Public replay and code rights

Kaggle staff publishes a daily Top Episodes dataset and documents CLI commands to list and download competition episodes. This establishes an official public replay channel, useful for observation and measurement. It does **not**, by itself, establish that a replay grants a license to copy a competitor's private implementation or that every form of replay-derived policy is permitted. The discussions do not resolve that question. [Kaggle staff: Top Episodes dataset announcement](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/kaggriculture/discussion/732114); [official Kaggriculture getting-started notebook](https://www.kaggle.com/code/bovard/kaggriculture-getting-started/input).

The Kaggle rules discussion quotes Rule 6.b: if a participant publicly shares Competition Code, it must be shared on the Kaggle competition forum/notebooks and is deemed licensed under an OSI-approved license permitting commercial use. Rule 6.a restricts private sharing of Competition Code between separate teams during the competition. Kaggle staff also posted a warning not to respond to emails claiming to be Kaggle admins asking for source code or personal information. This is a concrete reason to use only code explicitly public on Kaggle with its stated license and attribution, and never send our private agent to a stranger. It still does not settle replay-derived strategy rights; the binding competition Rules page is authoritative. [Rule 6 clarification thread](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/kaggriculture/discussion/733868); [Kaggle staff source-code scam warning](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/kaggriculture/discussion/737885); [competition Rules page](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/kaggriculture/rules).

## Recommendation for V62

Use current version-pinned official source as the mechanics contract. Audit action validity and tile production timing; instrument fertilizer use and market sales by item; then evaluate hypotheses as paired, two-seat live games with an untouched seed set and multiple real opponents. Keep replays for extracting what high performers do and how the game state evolves, but do not treat a fixed top replay as an adaptive opponent in counterfactual matches. Score candidate changes primarily by win rate and track coin margin/action efficiency as supporting explanations. No discussion post establishes a winning crop mix or a complete top-player policy; those must be recovered from actual replays and independently tested.
